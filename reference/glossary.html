A

Acceptable quality level (AQL)
The poorest fraction defective that a consumer would still accept as a supplier's process average. A sampling plan is designed to accept lots at the AQL most of the time. Module 9
Acceptance number
The largest number of defectives a sample may contain for the lot to be accepted, written c in a single sampling plan. Module 9
Acceptance sampling
Inspecting a random sample from a lot and using the result to accept or reject the whole lot. It judges product that has already been made and does nothing to improve the process. Module 9
Assignable cause
Shewhart's term for a special cause. It is a specific source of variation outside the usual process, such as a worn tool or a new lot of raw material. Module 1
Attribute data
Data that count or classify items, such as the number of defectives in a sample or the number of scratches on a panel, instead of measuring them on a continuous scale. Module 5
Autocorrelation
Correlation between an observation and earlier observations in the same series. Standard control limits assume there is none, so strongly autocorrelated data need other methods. Module 11
Average outgoing quality (AOQ)
The average fraction defective in lots after inspection, when rejected lots are screened and their defectives replaced. Module 9
Average outgoing quality limit (AOQL)
The highest value the AOQ reaches over all levels of incoming quality. It caps the average quality that leaves inspection. Module 9
Average run length (ARL)
The average number of points plotted before a chart signals. A good chart has a long ARL while the process is in control and a short one after a shift. Module 2
Average sample number (ASN)
The average number of items inspected per lot under a double, multiple or sequential plan, where the amount inspected depends on what the first items show. Module 9
Average total inspection (ATI)
The average number of items inspected per lot, counting the 100% screening of every rejected lot. Module 9

B

Batch process
A process that handles items in groups, such as wafers in a cassette, so items in the same group share conditions. Batches add layers of variation that a single control chart cannot separate. Module 12
Box plot
A graph of a group's median, middle half and extremes. Box plots side by side compare groups at a glance. Module 12
Box–Jenkins model
A time-series model, also called ARIMA, built from autoregressive terms, moving-average terms and differencing, and used to describe and forecast a series. Module 11

C

c chart
A control chart for the number of defects in inspection units of the same size. Its limits come from the Poisson distribution. Module 5
Capability
How well the natural spread of a stable process fits inside its specification limits, usually summarized by indices such as Cp and Cpk. Module 8
Center line
The line drawn at the average of the plotted statistic on a control chart. Module 2
Chance cause
Shewhart's term for a common cause, one of the many small influences that are always present in a process. Module 1
Common cause
A source of variation built into the process and present all the time. Only a change to the process itself reduces common-cause variation. Module 1
Consumer's risk
The probability that a sampling plan accepts a lot whose quality is at the LTPD, a lot the consumer considers bad. It is often written β. Module 9
Control chart
A time-ordered plot of a process statistic with a center line and control limits, used to separate the signals of special causes from common-cause noise. Module 2
Control limit
A line on a control chart, usually three standard deviations of the plotted statistic from the center line. A point beyond it signals that the process may have changed. Module 2
Cp
A capability index that compares the width of the specification with six process standard deviations. It ignores where the process is centered. Module 8
Cpk
A capability index based on the distance from the process mean to the nearer specification limit, in units of three standard deviations. An off-center process gets a lower Cpk. Module 8
Cpm
A capability index that measures spread around the target value instead of around the process mean, so it falls as the mean moves away from target. Module 8
CUSUM chart
A chart of cumulative sums of deviations from target, which lets a small, persistent shift build up into a clear signal. Module 6

D

Decision interval
The CUSUM threshold, written h. The chart signals when either cumulative sum exceeds it. Module 6
Defect
A single flaw on an item, such as a scratch or a void. One item can carry several defects. Module 5
Defective
An item that fails to meet its requirements, also called nonconforming. A defective item has at least one defect serious enough to reject it. Module 5
Double exponential smoothing
Exponential smoothing with a second equation for the trend, so forecasts can follow a series that rises or falls steadily. Module 11
Double sampling
A sampling plan that takes a second sample when the first one gives no clear decision to accept or reject. Module 9

E

EWMA chart
A chart of the exponentially weighted moving average, which gives the newest observation a weight λ and older ones geometrically less. It detects small shifts sooner than a Shewhart chart. Module 7
Exponential smoothing
A forecasting method in which each new smoothed value is a weighted average of the latest observation and the previous smoothed value. Module 11

F

False alarm
A control chart signal when the process has not changed. With 3σ limits and a stable, normally distributed process, about 0.27% of points give one. Module 2

H

Hotelling T²
A single statistic that measures how far a set of several measurements lies from its mean, taking the correlations between the measurements into account. The T² chart plots it against an upper limit. Module 10

I

In statistical control
Describes a process whose variation comes only from common causes, so that its future output is predictable within limits. Module 1
Individuals chart
A control chart for single measurements, with limits estimated from the moving ranges between neighbouring points. It is also called an X chart. Module 4

L

Lot
A group of items produced under similar conditions and judged together, such as a cassette of wafers or a shipment of parts. Module 9
Lot-to-lot variation
Variation between lot averages that is part of how a batch process normally runs. Limits for lot means must include it, as the LL limits in the lithography case study do. Module 12
LTPD
Lot tolerance percent defective, the fraction defective at which the consumer wants lots rejected with high probability. It is also called the rejectable quality level. Module 9

M

MEWMA chart
The multivariate EWMA chart. It smooths a vector of measurements over time and plots a T²-type distance of the smoothed vector. Module 10
Moving average
The average of the most recent k observations, recomputed as each new observation arrives. Module 11
Moving range
The absolute difference between two consecutive observations. The average moving range estimates short-term variation for an individuals chart. Module 4
Multivariate control chart
A chart that monitors several correlated measurements together through one statistic, such as Hotelling's T². Module 10

N

Nested design
A data structure in which each level of one factor appears within only one level of another, such as wafers inside cassettes. Wafer 1 of cassette 3 is unrelated to wafer 1 of cassette 7. Module 12
Nonconforming
Failing to meet a specification. A nonconforming item is a defective. Module 5
np chart
A control chart for the number of defectives in samples of a fixed size n. Module 5

O

OC curve
The operating characteristic curve of a sampling plan, which plots the probability of accepting a lot against the lot's true fraction defective. Module 9
OCAP
An out-of-control action plan, a flowchart written in advance for a control chart that tells people what to check, and in what order, when the chart signals. Module 12

P

p chart
A control chart for the fraction defective in samples, which may vary in size. Module 5
Phase I
The baseline stage of control charting. Historical data are charted with trial limits, points with assignable causes are removed, and the limits are recomputed until they describe the process running as it should. Module 1
Phase II
The monitoring stage, in which new data are plotted against the limits fixed in Phase I. Module 1
Principal components
New variables formed as uncorrelated weighted combinations of the original measurements, ordered by how much of the variation each explains. A few components can summarize many correlated measurements. Module 10
Producer's risk
The probability that a sampling plan rejects a lot whose quality is at the AQL, a lot the producer considers good. It is often written α. Module 9

R

R chart
A control chart of subgroup ranges that monitors process spread, usually drawn under an X̄ chart. Module 3
Random-effects ANOVA
An analysis of variance that treats the levels of a factor, such as cassettes, as a random sample from a larger population and estimates how much variance each factor contributes. Module 12
Rational subgroup
A subgroup chosen so that only common-cause variation acts within it, which makes any difference between subgroups stand out. Module 3
Rectifying inspection
Inspection in which rejected lots are screened 100% and their defectives replaced, which improves the quality that leaves inspection. Module 9
Run rules
Extra rules that flag unusual patterns inside the control limits, such as several points in a row on one side of the center line. Module 2

S

s chart
A control chart of subgroup standard deviations that monitors process spread. It is preferred to the R chart for larger subgroups. Module 3
Sequential sampling
A sampling plan that inspects items one at a time and decides after each one whether to accept the lot, reject it, or keep sampling. Module 9
Shewhart chart
The classic control chart, which plots each subgroup's statistic on its own against 3σ limits around a center line. Module 2
Single sampling plan
A plan that takes one sample of n items and accepts the lot if it contains c or fewer defectives. Module 9
Skip-lot sampling
A plan that inspects only a fraction of lots once a supplier has built a good record, and returns to inspecting every lot when one fails. Module 9
Smoothing constant
The weight that an EWMA or exponential smoothing gives to the newest observation, written λ or α. Small values average over a long memory, and values near 1 react quickly. Module 7
Special cause
A specific, identifiable source of variation that is not part of the usual process, such as a mis-set dial or a bad batch of material. Special causes show up as signals on a control chart. Module 1
Specification limit
A limit on an individual item, set by the design or the customer. Specification limits are the voice of the customer and are set independently of control limits. Module 1
Stratification
Mixing items from different sources, such as several machines or mold cavities, in one subgroup. The subgroup spread grows, the limits widen, and the points hug the center line. Module 3
Subgroup
A small set of measurements taken close together in time and plotted as one point on a control chart. Module 3

T

Tampering
Adjusting a stable process in response to common-cause variation. Each adjustment adds variation instead of removing it. Module 1

U

u chart
A control chart for defects per unit when the amount inspected varies from sample to sample. Module 5

V

V-mask
A V-shaped template laid over a CUSUM plot. The process is out of control when earlier points fall outside the arms of the V. Module 6
Variance component
The part of the total variance that comes from one source, such as differences between cassettes, estimated with a random-effects analysis of variance. Module 12

W

Western Electric rules
The run rules published in Western Electric's 1956 quality control handbook, based on zones one, two and three standard deviations from the center line. Module 2

X

X̄ chart
A control chart of subgroup averages that monitors the process mean. Its limits come from the variation within subgroups. Module 3

Z

Zones
Bands one, two and three standard deviations from the center line, labelled C, B and A, that the Western Electric rules use. Module 2
