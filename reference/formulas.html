Variables charts

Explained in module 3, X̄ and R or s charts, and module 4, individuals and moving range charts.

X̄ and R chart

\begin{aligned} \text{UCL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} + A_2\bar{R} & \text{UCL}_R &= D_4\bar{R} \\ \text{CL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} & \text{CL}_R &= \bar{R} \\ \text{LCL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} - A_2\bar{R} & \text{LCL}_R &= D_3\bar{R} \end{aligned}
\hat\sigma = \frac{\bar{R}}{d_2}, \qquad A_2 = \frac{3}{d_2\sqrt{n}}, \qquad D_3 = \max\!\left(0,\ 1 - \frac{3d_3}{d_2}\right), \qquad D_4 = 1 + \frac{3d_3}{d_2}
\bar{\bar{x}}
grand mean, the average of the subgroup means
\bar{R}
average subgroup range
n
subgroup size
d_2,\ d_3
mean and standard deviation of the range of n standard normal values

X̄ and s chart

\begin{aligned} \text{UCL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} + A_3\bar{s} & \text{UCL}_s &= B_4\bar{s} \\ \text{CL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} & \text{CL}_s &= \bar{s} \\ \text{LCL}_{\bar{x}} &= \bar{\bar{x}} - A_3\bar{s} & \text{LCL}_s &= B_3\bar{s} \end{aligned}
\hat\sigma = \frac{\bar{s}}{c_4}, \qquad A_3 = \frac{3}{c_4\sqrt{n}}, \qquad B_3,\ B_4 = 1 \mp \frac{3\sqrt{1-c_4^2}}{c_4}, \qquad c_4 = \sqrt{\frac{2}{n-1}}\;\frac{\Gamma(n/2)}{\Gamma\big((n-1)/2\big)}
\bar{s}
average subgroup standard deviation
c_4
the ratio E[s]/\sigma for samples of size n from a normal population
B_3
set to 0 when the formula gives a negative value

Individuals and moving range chart

\text{MR}_i = \left|x_i - x_{i-1}\right|, \qquad \overline{MR} = \frac{1}{m-1}\sum_{i=2}^{m}\text{MR}_i
\begin{aligned} \text{UCL}_x &= \bar{x} + 3\,\frac{\overline{MR}}{1.128} = \bar{x} + 2.66\,\overline{MR} & \text{UCL}_{MR} &= 3.267\,\overline{MR} \\ \text{CL}_x &= \bar{x} & \text{CL}_{MR} &= \overline{MR} \\ \text{LCL}_x &= \bar{x} - 2.66\,\overline{MR} & \text{LCL}_{MR} &= 0 \end{aligned}
m
number of individual observations
1.128,\ 3.267
d_2 and D_4 for n = 2, because each moving range spans two observations

Western Electric run rules

Explained in module 2, how control charts work.

The zones split each side of the center line into thirds: zone C lies within 1σ of the center line, zone B between 1σ and 2σ, and zone A between 2σ and 3σ. Here σ is the standard deviation of the plotted statistic. A point completes a signal when any of these patterns appears.

    Two trend rules are often added:

      Every extra rule shortens the time to detect a shift and also adds false alarms, so the in-control average run length falls as rules are added.

      Average run length of a 3σ chart

      Explained in module 2, how control charts work.

      \text{ARL} = \frac{1}{p}, \qquad p = \Phi(-3 - \delta) + 1 - \Phi(3 - \delta)
      p
      probability that one point falls outside the limits
      \delta
      shift in the mean, in standard deviations of the plotted statistic
      \Phi
      standard normal cumulative distribution function

      An X̄ chart with subgroups of n sees a process shift of k\sigma as a shift of k\sqrt{n} standard deviations of the subgroup mean, which is why larger subgroups catch shifts sooner. The table uses rule 1 only.

      CUSUM

      Explained in module 6, CUSUM charts.

      Tabular CUSUM

      \begin{aligned} S_{hi}(i) &= \max\left(0,\ S_{hi}(i-1) + x_i - \hat\mu_0 - k\right) \\ S_{lo}(i) &= \max\left(0,\ S_{lo}(i-1) + \hat\mu_0 - k - x_i\right) \end{aligned}

      Both sums start at 0. The process is out of control when S_{hi}(i) > h or S_{lo}(i) > h.

      \hat\mu_0
      target or in-control mean
      k
      reference value, usually half the shift to detect
      h
      decision interval, often 4σ or 5σ

      V-mask design

      S_m = \sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\bar{x}_i - \hat\mu_0\right)
      k = \frac{\delta\sigma}{2}, \qquad d = \frac{2}{\delta^2}\ln\!\left(\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}\right), \qquad h = d\,k
      \delta
      shift to detect, in standard deviations of the plotted values
      \alpha
      probability of a false alarm
      \beta
      probability of missing a shift of size \delta
      d
      lead distance: how far ahead of the latest point the mask's vertex sits
      k,\ h
      slope of the mask's arms per sample, and their rise above the vertex line at the latest point

      EWMA

      Explained in module 7, EWMA charts.

      z_t = \lambda x_t + (1-\lambda)\,z_{t-1}, \qquad z_0 = \mu_0
      \text{UCL}_t,\ \text{LCL}_t = \mu_0 \pm L\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2-\lambda}\left[1-(1-\lambda)^{2t}\right]}
      \text{as } t \to \infty: \quad \mu_0 \pm L\sigma\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{2-\lambda}}
      \lambda
      smoothing constant, 0 < \lambda \le 1; values of 0.2 to 0.3 are common, and \lambda = 1 gives a Shewhart chart
      L
      limit width, usually 3 (the handbook writes k)
      \sigma
      standard deviation of the plotted observations

      The exact limits start narrow and widen toward the steady-state width within a few points. The handbook's example uses the steady-state limits throughout.

      Capability indices

      Explained in module 8, process capability.

      \begin{aligned} C_p &= \frac{USL - LSL}{6\sigma} & C_{pk} &= \min\left(C_{pu},\ C_{pl}\right) \\ C_{pu} &= \frac{USL - \mu}{3\sigma} & C_{pl} &= \frac{\mu - LSL}{3\sigma} \end{aligned}
      C_{pm} = \frac{USL - LSL}{6\sqrt{\sigma^2 + (\mu - T)^2}}
      USL,\ LSL
      upper and lower specification limits
      \mu,\ \sigma
      process mean and standard deviation, estimated by \bar{x} and s (or \hat\sigma from a control chart) once the process is stable
      T
      target value

      With a one-sided specification, use C_{pu} or C_{pl} alone.

      Acceptance sampling

      Explained in module 9, lot acceptance sampling.

      Single sampling plan (n, c)

      P_a = \sum_{d=0}^{c}\binom{n}{d}\,p^d\,(1-p)^{n-d}
      \text{AOQ} = \frac{P_a\,p\,(N-n)}{N}, \qquad \text{AOQL} = \max_{p}\ \text{AOQ}, \qquad \text{ATI} = n + (1-P_a)(N-n)
      N,\ n,\ c
      lot size, sample size and acceptance number
      p
      fraction defective in the incoming lots
      P_a
      probability of accepting a lot; plotted against p it is the OC curve

      AOQ and ATI assume that rejected lots are inspected 100% and that every defective found is replaced with a good item.

      Double sampling plan

      P_a = P(d_1 \le c_1) + \sum_{d_1 = c_1 + 1}^{r_1 - 1} P(d_1)\,P(d_2 \le c_2 - d_1)
      \text{ASN} = n_1 P_1 + (n_1 + n_2)(1 - P_1) = n_1 + n_2(1 - P_1)
      c_1,\ r_1
      accept on the first sample if d_1 \le c_1, reject if d_1 \ge r_1, otherwise take the second sample
      c_2
      accept after the second sample if d_1 + d_2 \le c_2
      P_1
      probability of reaching a decision on the first sample

      Hotelling T²

      Explained in module 10, multivariate control charts.

      T_j^2 = n\,\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j - \bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\right)^{\top}\mathbf{S}_p^{-1}\left(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_j - \bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}\right)
      \begin{aligned} \text{Phase I: } \text{UCL} &= \frac{p(k-1)(n-1)}{kn - k - p + 1}\,F_{\alpha;\,p,\,kn-k-p+1} \\ \text{Phase II: } \text{UCL} &= \frac{p(k+1)(n-1)}{kn - k - p + 1}\,F_{\alpha;\,p,\,kn-k-p+1} \end{aligned}
      \bar{\mathbf{x}}_j
      vector of the p variable means in subgroup j
      \bar{\bar{\mathbf{x}}}
      vector of grand means
      \mathbf{S}_p
      pooled covariance matrix, the average of the subgroup covariance matrices
      p,\ k,\ n
      number of variables, number of Phase I subgroups, subgroup size
      F_{\alpha;\,\nu_1,\,\nu_2}
      upper \alpha point of the F distribution

      The T² chart has no lower limit, because T² measures distance and cannot be negative.

      Exponential smoothing

      Explained in module 11, time series and forecasting.

      Single exponential smoothing

      S_t = \alpha\,y_{t-1} + (1-\alpha)\,S_{t-1}, \qquad 0 < \alpha \le 1, \qquad S_2 = y_1

      In the handbook's convention S_t uses data up to period t-1, so it is the forecast for period t.

      Double exponential smoothing

      \begin{aligned} S_t &= \alpha\,y_t + (1-\alpha)\left(S_{t-1} + b_{t-1}\right) \\ b_t &= \gamma\left(S_t - S_{t-1}\right) + (1-\gamma)\,b_{t-1} \\ F_{t+m} &= S_t + m\,b_t \end{aligned}
      S_t,\ b_t
      smoothed level and smoothed trend at period t; start with S_1 = y_1 and b_1 = y_2 - y_1
      \alpha,\ \gamma
      smoothing constants for the level and the trend, each between 0 and 1
      F_{t+m}
      forecast m periods ahead
